Starting Your Journey!

Build a Strong Project Partnership

Establishing professional communication, conducting initial meetings, and finalizing partnership agreements.

Value & Narrative

First impressions shape the trajectory of a partnership. The starting phase is where abstract project ideas turn into structured, professional commitments. By establishing clear team contracts, co-creating agendas, and aligning on project scope early, you build psychological safety within your team and trust with your client. Learning how to navigate early ambiguity is one of the most valuable professional capabilities you will gain at UMSI.

Focus & Objectives

  • Communication: Draft clear, professional outreach to launch working relationships.
  • First Meetings: Structure productive kickoff meetings that define roles and project bounds.
  • Formalizing Agreements: Document team commitments and scope agreements shared by all stakeholders.

Your First Steps

Use this checklist to move from a new project idea to a confirmed partnership. Each step links to a tool you can use right away.

  1. Reach out to your partner. Adapt the Initial Client Email Examples to introduce your team and propose a first meeting time.
  2. Plan the kickoff meeting. Use the Initial Client Meeting Sample Agenda to decide what you will cover and who will lead each part.
  3. Learn the partnership principles. Read the Ginsberg Center Student-Client Partnership Toolkit and the Student-Client Partnership Guide before you commit to a scope or deliverables.
  4. Put agreements in writing. Student organization teams can use the Student Org-ELO Partnership Agreement Template to confirm roles between your team and the ELO.
  5. Mark your key dates. Check the SO-ELL Schedule for milestones, deadlines, and submission dates.

Make Your First Meeting Count

The kickoff meeting is where your team and your partner agree on what you will work on, who is responsible for what, and how you will stay in touch. Share an agenda ahead of time, take notes during the conversation, and leave with next steps that everyone can review.

Questions to Bring

  • What does success look like for your organization?
  • Who will be our main contact, and how often should we meet?
  • What limits, such as time, tools, policies, or budget, should we plan around?
  • How would you like to receive progress updates and give feedback?
  • Which parts of the project are most important to finish first?

After the Meeting

Send a short thank-you message that summarizes what you agreed on, including roles, scope, and next steps. If anything changes later, update your written agreement so your team and your partner stay aligned.
